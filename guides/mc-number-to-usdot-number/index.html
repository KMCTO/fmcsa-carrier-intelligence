How to find a USDOT number from an MC number

Last updated: November 9, 2026. General information, not legal advice.

A rate confirmation gives you an MC number. Most FMCSA safety records are filed under the USDOT number. Here is how to get from one to the other, and what to check on the way.

The short answer

Enter the MC number on the free lookup page. It returns the USDOT number of the carrier that holds that docket, the docket's status, and the date those records are current as of. No account and no payment are needed. FMCSA's own SAFER site also searches by MC number.

The lookup accepts the spellings you will see in the wild: MC12892, MC-012892, 12892 and mc 12892 all find the same docket.

MC number or USDOT number: what each one is

  • A USDOT number identifies a company registered with FMCSA. Roadside inspections, crashes and the safety record are filed under it, so it is the number most safety data uses.
  • An MC, MX or FF number identifies a docket, a file of operating authority: MC for motor carriers and brokers, MX for Mexico-domiciled carriers, and FF for freight forwarders.
  • The two are not one-to-one. A carrier can have a USDOT number and no docket, for example one that hauls only its own freight. A carrier can also hold more than one docket.

Check these four things after the lookup

  1. Does the USDOT number match your paperwork? Compare it with the number on the carrier's setup packet, insurance certificate and rate confirmation. A mismatch is a reason to look closer. It is not proof of anything by itself.
  2. What is the docket status? Active, Inactive and similar values describe the docket, not the carrier's whole business.
  3. How current is the answer? Every result states its as-of date. FMCSA stopped updating its legacy licensing archive on May 14, 2026, so for many carriers the status describes that date. See what the May 14 freeze means for carrier vetting.
  4. Does the carrier link to others? With the USDOT number in hand, the free identity screen shows whether the carrier shares contact identifiers with other registrations. See chameleon carriers.

Why the same docket looks different in different files

FMCSA's files write docket numbers with and without leading zeros and hyphens, and some dockets appear in the census under a second carrier. The lookup normalizes the spelling and reports the carrier that holds the authority. If a docket is listed under more than one carrier, the result says so.

Common questions

Is an MC number the same as a USDOT number?

No. A USDOT number identifies a company registered with FMCSA and is the number safety records are filed under. An MC number identifies a docket of operating authority. A carrier can have a USDOT number and no MC number, and can hold more than one docket.

How do I find a carrier's USDOT number from its MC number?

Enter the MC number on the free lookup page at carriers.4sakeco.com/lookup/. It returns the USDOT number of the carrier holding that docket, the docket status and the date the records are current as of. FMCSA's SAFER site also searches by MC number.

Does the lookup cost anything?

No. The MC-to-USDOT lookup and the identity screen are free and need no account. Tools that return full carrier records are paid per call, from $0.01.

Is the docket status live?

No. Each answer states the date its records are current as of. For many carriers the authority status comes from an FMCSA archive that stopped updating on May 14, 2026, so confirm current authority with FMCSA before relying on it.
