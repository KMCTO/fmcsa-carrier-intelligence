What you get

Authority, insurance and safety

Operating authority, insurance filings, out-of-service orders, the FMCSA safety rating and its date, and inspection and crash summaries. Every answer says how current it is.

Identity-linkage screen

A free screen shows whether a carrier shares contact identifiers with other registrations, a pattern worth a closer look when a carrier re-registers under a new number. A flag is a data pattern, not a finding of fraud.

Records you can keep

A carrier selection record is a dated document with a SHA-256 hash of its contents, and a free link confirms we issued exactly that record. Read what a carrier-selection file should show after Montgomery.

Who it's for

Freight brokers, shippers and 3PLs vetting carriers before a load is tendered: catching double-brokering and carrier-identity theft, checking that a carrier's operating authority and insurance filings are on file, and checking whether a new applicant links back to a carrier already removed from your network. It describes regulated motor carriers as business entities. It is not a consumer report and must not be used as one; see the Terms.

About the data

The data comes from FMCSA's public open-data releases (the carrier census, licensing and insurance filings, out-of-service orders, revocations, SMS safety measures and crash records). It is refreshed regularly, and every response states the date its records are current as of. One caveat matters: FMCSA stopped updating its legacy licensing and insurance archives on 2026-05-14, so for about 72% of Active-authority carriers the authority and insurance-on-file fields describe that date (these carry the flag authority_status_is_legacy_li_not_motus). FMCSA builds its own snapshots from a database up to 24 hours old and states that it does not reflect real-time data, so treat any answer as of its stated date, never as live.
